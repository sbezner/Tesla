Tesla, Inc.

A Brief History

2003 - The Beginning

Tesla Motors was founded in July 2003 by engineers Martin Eberhard and Marc Tarpenning in San Carlos, California. The company was named after inventor Nikola Tesla, whose alternating current motors inspired the company's electric vehicle technology.

2004 - Musk Joins

Elon Musk joined as chairman of the board and lead investor in February 2004, contributing most of the initial $7.5 million Series A funding. Musk became deeply involved in the company's product design and engineering decisions.

2008 - The Roadster

Tesla launched its first vehicle, the Roadster, a sports car that proved electric vehicles could be both high-performance and desirable. It could travel over 200 miles on a single charge, challenging perceptions about electric vehicle limitations.

2010 - Going Public

Tesla went public on NASDAQ in June 2010, raising $226 million in its initial public offering. It was the first American car company to go public since Ford Motor Company in 1956.

2012 - Model S

The Model S luxury sedan launched, winning numerous automotive awards including Motor Trend Car of the Year. This marked Tesla's transition from niche sports cars to broader market vehicles.

2015 - Energy Products

Tesla expanded beyond vehicles, announcing the Powerwall home battery and Powerpack utility-scale battery products. This move signaled the company's broader mission to accelerate sustainable energy adoption.

2015 - Model X

The Model X SUV was introduced, featuring distinctive falcon-wing doors and becoming one of the safest SUVs ever tested.

2017 - Model 3

The Model 3, Tesla's mass-market sedan, began production. Priced more affordably than previous models, it aimed to make electric vehicles accessible to a broader audience and quickly became one of the best-selling electric cars worldwide.

2020 - Model Y

The Model Y compact SUV launched, sharing much of its platform with the Model 3. It targeted the popular crossover SUV segment and became one of Tesla's best-selling vehicles.

Today

Tesla continues to produce electric vehicles, battery energy storage systems, and solar products. The company operates manufacturing facilities in multiple countries and remains a leading force in the transition to sustainable transportation and energy.
